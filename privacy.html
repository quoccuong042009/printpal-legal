The short version

PrintPal has no server and no account system. There is nothing to sign up for with us. The documents, photos and scans you print, fax or edit stay on your iPhone and go only to the printer you choose. We cannot see them, because they are never sent to us.

To find and fix problems, the app sends crash reports and anonymous usage statistics to Google’s Firebase. They never include your files or what is in them, file names, fax numbers, or the names and addresses of your printers, and they are not linked to your name or email. See Crash reports and usage statistics.

We do not sell or share personal data, we do not use advertising software, and we do not track you across other apps or websites.

How printing, scanning and faxing work

  • Finding printers. The app looks for printers on the Wi‑Fi network your iPhone is joined to and talks to them directly, over your local network. iOS asks your permission the first time. A printer you add by its address is contacted the same way.
  • Printing. What is sent to a printer is the document you chose to print, and it goes only to that printer. It does not pass through us.
  • Scanning. The camera is used only while you scan a document. The pages become a PDF on your iPhone.
  • Photos. When you print a photo, iOS lets the app see only the photos you pick. The app has no access to the rest of your library.
  • Fax. A fax is handed to a fax‑capable printer on your network, which dials it over its own telephone line. The pages do not pass through us or any third‑party fax service. The call itself is handled by the printer’s telephone provider.
  • Web pages. When you print a web page, your iPhone fetches that page directly from the website, as a browser would. The website sees that request as it would any visit.
  • Signatures. A signature you draw or type is drawn into the PDF you sign. It is not stored anywhere else, and not sent anywhere.

Crash reports and usage statistics

The app uses two parts of Google’s Firebase:

  • Crashlytics. If the app crashes, it sends a crash report: where in the app’s code the crash happened, the app and iOS versions, and the device model. We use these to fix the crash.
  • Analytics. The app sends anonymous usage statistics, such as which screens are opened, how long the app is used for, that a print, scan, fax or document tool was used and roughly how many pages it involved (as a range, such as “1–9”, never the exact number), whether a printer was found on the network or added by its address, the app and iOS versions, the device model, and an approximate region that Google works out from the internet connection. We use these to understand which parts of the app work and which need improving. Analytics runs with its advertising features switched off.

Both are tied only to a random identifier that Firebase creates for this installation of the app. It is not your name, your email or the advertising identifier, and deleting the app discards it. Neither includes your files, their names or contents, fax numbers, or your printers’ names or addresses. We do not use this information for advertising, and it is not used to track you. Google processes it on our behalf under Google’s privacy policy.

Removing your data

  • Delete a file by pressing and holding it in Files, or delete them all in Settings → Storage.
  • Remove a printer from its details screen.
  • Delete a fax from Fax → History by pressing and holding it.
  • Delete the app to remove everything it stored on your device.

We hold no account or profile data about you. Crash reports and usage statistics are tied only to the random installation identifier described above, and deleting the app starts a new one. If you would like crash reports or usage statistics removed, contact us and we will pass the request to Google.
